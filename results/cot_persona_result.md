Executive Recommendation

The company should invest the $50,000 in improving the existing product, with priority given to performance, reliability, and user experience.

This option best addresses the company's immediate weaknesses while creating a stronger foundation for sustainable revenue growth.

1. Current Situation Analysis

There are four important factors:

Customer satisfaction: Existing customers are reporting performance problems.
Customer growth: Growth has slowed over the past six months.
Development capacity: The current team is small, limiting how quickly improvements can be delivered.
Financial stability: The company has only one major $50,000 investment available, so the decision should produce meaningful long-term value.

The product-performance complaints are particularly important because they can affect both customer retention and future growth.

2. Option Analysis
Option 1 — Invest in Marketing

Potential benefits:

Can increase brand awareness and attract new customers.
Could generate additional revenue.
May help address the recent slowdown in customer acquisition.

Risks:

Marketing may bring more customers to a product that currently has performance problems.
Poor user experience could increase dissatisfaction and churn.
The company could spend money acquiring customers without solving the underlying product issue.

Overall: Marketing could be valuable later, but it is not the strongest first investment given the current product problems.

Option 2 — Hire Two Developers

Potential benefits:

Increases development capacity.
Allows the company to work on improvements and new features faster.
Reduces pressure on the existing development team.
Creates additional capacity for future growth.

Risks:

Hiring creates ongoing costs beyond the initial investment.
New developers need time to understand the company's product and codebase.
More developers do not automatically solve the existing performance and reliability problems.
The $50,000 may not provide enough long-term financial flexibility for two additional employees.

Overall: Hiring could become valuable as the company grows, but it is less directly aligned with the company's immediate problem.

Option 3 — Improve the Existing Product

Potential benefits:

Directly addresses existing customer complaints.
Improves performance and reliability.
Can increase customer satisfaction and retention.
Creates a stronger user experience.
A more reliable product provides a better foundation for future marketing.
Better product quality can support long-term revenue growth.

Risks:

The company may not see an immediate increase in new customers.
Development work may temporarily delay new features.
The company must carefully prioritize which improvements provide the greatest business value.

Overall: This option addresses the most immediate weakness while strengthening the company's long-term position.

Final Recommendation

Invest the $50,000 in improving the existing product.

The company should first address performance, reliability, and user-experience problems because these issues directly affect customer satisfaction and potentially contribute to the slowdown in customer growth.

Once the product becomes more stable and reliable, the company will have a stronger foundation for marketing and customer acquisition. Hiring additional developers can also become a future priority once the company has stronger growth and clearer evidence that additional development capacity is required.

Recommended strategy

Improve the product → increase customer satisfaction and retention → strengthen the business foundation → then scale marketing and development capacity.

This approach provides the best balance between customer satisfaction, product quality, revenue potential, and financial stability.
